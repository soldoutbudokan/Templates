# Penne with Spicy Sausage and Tomato Cream

Brian Lagerstrom's baked one-pot pasta, made with penne and a heavy hand of spicy Italian sausage. The pasta cooks in the sauce in the oven, and the sausage brings all the heat.

**Prep Time:** 15 minutes
**Cook Time:** 50 minutes, plus 10–15 minutes resting
**Servings:** 8

## Ingredients

- 1.3 tbsp olive oil
- 900g spicy Italian sausage, casings removed
- 1 large yellow onion, grated on a box grater
- 5.3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1.7 tbsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt, divided
- 1 large can (796 mL / 28 oz) crushed tomatoes
- 1.3 tsp granulated sugar
- 1.5 L water
- 500g penne (one package), bronze-cut if you can find it
- 100 mL heavy/whipping cream (35%)
- 0.5 cups Parmesan, finely grated and packed, plus more for serving
- 0.2 cups fresh basil, finely chopped
- 0.3 tsp black pepper, freshly cracked
- 0.7 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, for drizzling

## Instructions

1. **Preheat the oven:** Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C) with a rack in the middle. Use a 6–7 qt Dutch oven; a 5 qt will be too full at this size.

2. **Brown the sausage:** Heat the Dutch oven over medium-high and add the olive oil. Add the sausage and cook 6–8 minutes, breaking it into fine, even crumbles with a meat masher or wooden spoon, until lightly browned and the fat has rendered. If bits stick hard to the bottom, splash in a tablespoon of water and scrape.

3. **Sweat the aromatics:** Add the grated onion, garlic, and half of the salt. Cook, stirring, 6–7 minutes, until the onion melts into the sausage and the garlic is soft and fragrant.

4. **Build the sauce:** Add the crushed tomatoes, sugar, the rest of the salt, and the water. Stir well, scraping the bottom, and bring to a gentle simmer.

5. **Add pasta and bake:** Stir in the penne and press it down so it's fully submerged. Turn off the burner and move the pot, uncovered, to the oven. Bake 20 minutes, pulling it out at 5, 10, and 15 minutes to stir and scrape the bottom so nothing sticks.

6. **Check the pasta:** Penne cooks a little faster than rigatoni, so start tasting at the 15-minute stir. You want firm al dente; it softens while it rests. If it's still too firm or the sauce has dried out, stir in about ½ cup hot water and bake 3–5 minutes more.

7. **Finish with cream and cheese:** Take the pot out. Add the cream, Parmesan, and basil, and fold gently with slow, wide strokes. Taste a noodle with sauce and add a pinch or two of salt if needed.

8. **Rest:** Let it sit uncovered in the pot for 10–15 minutes. The starch thickens the sauce so it clings to the pasta.

9. **Serve:** Spoon into shallow bowls, scooping the chunky sauce from the edges over the top. Finish with extra Parmesan, the black pepper, and a drizzle of extra-virgin olive oil.

## Notes

Pasta quantity: 600g of penne would balance this much sausage and water better, but packages come in 500g. One package works fine; the result is just a bit saucier and meatier.

Scaling: the original ratio is 600g sausage and 1 L water to 453g pasta, which makes about 4 servings. This version uses 900g sausage and 1.5 L water with the other ingredients unchanged, and comes out to about 8 servings.
